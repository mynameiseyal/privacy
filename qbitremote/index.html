QBitRemote — Privacy Policy

Effective date: 29 October 2026

QBitRemote lets you control qBittorrent on your own computer from your Android phone. It has two parts: the app on your phone and the agent, a small program you install on your computer. This policy explains what each part stores, what travels between them, and what we can and can't see. The app also shows ads, and ads involve Google collecting some information about your phone; that's covered below too.

Short version

  • No accounts. QBitRemote never asks who you are.
  • Your torrents stay between your phone and your computer. We don't receive your torrent list, file names or anything you add.
  • Everything between phone and computer is end-to-end encrypted. Away from home it passes through our relay, which only forwards encrypted bytes it can't read.
  • The app shows ads through Google AdMob. Google receives your phone's advertising ID and technical data to serve them. We run no analytics of our own.
  • You can remove the ads with a one-time in-app purchase.
  • The camera is used only to scan the pairing QR code, on your phone. Images are never stored or sent anywhere.
  • Sites you add open in an in-app browser. Your logins stay on your phone; QBitRemote never stores your site passwords and we never see them.

Data stored on your phone

In the app's private storage:

  • the phone's own encryption key, itself encrypted with a key held in the Android Keystore;
  • the computer you paired with: its public key, ID, name and network addresses, and the relay address;
  • the sites you added under Your sites: the name and address you gave each one;
  • cookies those sites set when you log in, kept by the in-app browser (Android WebView) so you stay logged in. The app never stores your site usernames or passwords; Android Autofill or your password manager can fill them in, as in any browser.

When you tap a .torrent link in a site, the app downloads the file from that site with its cookies and sends it, end-to-end encrypted, to your computer. Magnet links, and links or files you share to QBitRemote from other apps, are sent the same way. Pages you visit go directly between your phone and the site, as in any browser; they don't pass through us. Google Safe Browsing checks the addresses you visit for known dangerous sites.

This data is excluded from Android backups and device-to-device transfers. To delete it, choose Forget this computer in the app, clear the app's data, or uninstall it.

Data stored on your computer

The agent keeps, on your computer only: its own keys, the list of phones you paired (each phone's public key, a scrambled identifier that lets it recognise the same phone after a reinstall, and the name you see in the app, such as "Samsung SM-S931B", with when it paired and was last seen), and the password it uses for qBittorrent's WebUI. Remove a phone with sudo qbitremote-agent devices revoke "<name>"; remove everything with sudo apt purge qbitremote-agent.

What travels over the network

ConnectionWhat goes over itWho can read it
Phone ↔ computer on your home network Your torrent list and your commands (pause, add, delete…) Only your phone and your computer. It's encrypted end to end (Noise protocol) with keys exchanged by the QR code.
Computer → your home network An mDNS announcement with the agent's random ID and the computer's name, so the app can find it Devices on your home network, like any network printer or speaker
Phone ↔ relay ↔ computer, away from home The same encrypted messages Only your phone and your computer. The relay sees the computer's random ID, the connecting IP addresses, and the size and timing of encrypted messages, but not their content. It stores none of this.
Phone ↔ Google (AdMob, Play) Ad requests with the data listed under Advertising; the "Remove ads" purchase and its status Google, under its own privacy policy. Nothing about your torrents, computer or sites is included.
Your computer ↔ our download site The installer and updates (through apt) Public files; like any website, the hosting provider receives the request's IP address.

The relay and the download site run on Cloudflare. Cloudflare processes connection data (such as IP addresses) to operate its network; see Cloudflare's Privacy Policy. We keep no logs of relay traffic.

Advertising

The app shows a banner ad at the bottom of the torrent list and, at most once a day when you open it, a full-screen app-open ad. These are served by Google AdMob.

To serve and measure ads, and to detect fraud, Google and its ad partners collect and process the following. This list follows Google's own disclosure for the Mobile Ads SDK:

  • Identifiers: your phone's Android advertising ID (which you can reset or delete) and its app set ID;
  • IP address, which can be used to estimate your phone's general location;
  • Product interactions: app launches, taps and video views;
  • Diagnostic information: app and SDK performance, such as launch time, hang rate and energy usage.

Google states these are used for advertising, analytics and fraud prevention, and that the data is encrypted in transit.

This information goes to Google, not to us. We see only aggregate, anonymous reports (for example, "how many ads were shown yesterday"), never anything that identifies you or your phone. Your torrents, your computer, the sites you added and their cookies are never shared with Google or any ad partner; the ads SDK has no access to them, and it isn't shown inside the in-app browser.

See Google's Privacy Policy and How Google uses information from sites or apps that use its services.

Your choices about ads

ChoiceWhat it does
Consent (EEA, UK, Switzerland) Where the law requires it, the app shows a Google consent form when you open it, and no ad loads until you answer. You can choose non-personalized ads, based on context rather than a profile of your interests. Change your answer any time in Settings → Privacy options.
Opt out of sale or sharing (US states) If you live in a US state with a privacy law covering this, such as California, Colorado, Connecticut, Texas or Virginia, you can tell us not to "sell" or "share" your personal information for targeted advertising, in Settings → Privacy options. Ads continue, but are no longer targeted using your information.
Reset or delete your advertising ID Android lets you reset the advertising ID, or delete it so apps receive none: Settings → Privacy → Ads on your phone (the path varies by device).
Remove ads A one-time in-app purchase removes all ads. Google Play handles the payment; we never receive your name, card or billing details. The app only learns from Play that you own the purchase, and asks Play again on each launch so it carries over to a reinstall or a new phone.

Permissions

  • Camera: scanning the pairing QR code. Optional: you can paste the pairing link instead.
  • Internet and network state: reaching your computer at home or through the relay, and noticing when you switch between Wi-Fi and mobile data.
  • Advertising ID (added by the Google Mobile Ads SDK): serving and measuring ads, as described above.
  • Google Play Billing: the optional "Remove ads" purchase.

Children

QBitRemote is not directed at children under 13, and we don't knowingly collect data from them. Its ad settings don't treat users as children.

Changes

If this policy changes, the new version will be posted here with a new effective date before that version is released. Changes to advertising or data sharing are also reflected in the app's Google Play Data safety section.
